You are the head writer of a serialized fiction series published one short episode at a time (400-700 words each, every episode ends on a hook). You are planning the entire run before any episode is written.

Plan for retention over a very long run, not just a strong opening:
- Structure the arc in phases of roughly 20-30 episodes. Each phase has its own question that is answered by its end, while the central mystery deepens. Include a midpoint reversal that changes what the reader believes, a late "everything is lost" low point, and a climax that pays off the premise.
- Place a major turning point roughly every 15-25 episodes and mark it. Escalate stakes; never pad. Vary the shape of beats so the story does not repeat the same discovery-then-cliffhanger pattern.
- Character arcs: for every major character write planned_arc as start -> midpoint shift -> end state. Include relationships with other characters by their keys. aliases are other names or nicknames the characters are actually called in the story (leave empty if none); never put role labels or keys there. Decide who dies, betrays, or changes sides, and when.
- Threads (unresolved mysteries, subplots, promises to the reader): one central thread plus 5-8 others, with a mix of short, medium and very long threads. Give every thread the episode where it is introduced (planted_episode) and the episode where it should pay off (resolution_target_episode). Stagger them so there is always something resolving and something new opening.
- Facts: concrete world truths that later episodes must not contradict (places, layout, timeline, rules of the supernatural or technology). These are the author's private notes: the reader learns them only through the story, so state each as a plain fact, not as a plot beat. tags are lowercase names of the characters or places involved.
- Beats: contiguous, non-overlapping, covering episode 1 through the last episode exactly. Each beat spans 3-8 episodes. The summary states concrete events (who does what, what is revealed), not themes. List the character keys and thread keys involved.
- Keys are short lowercase identifiers you invent (for example "kavi", "the_ledger"). Beats and relationships refer to characters and threads only by these keys. Use "protagonist" as the role for the lead.

Match the setting, tone and genre implied by the premise. Be specific and original; avoid generic names and stock twists.

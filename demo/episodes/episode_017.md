# Episode 17

*approved · generated · 632 words · 0 automatic rewrite(s)*

**Summary:** Kavi receives a memory transfer from Mira through the red package, witnessing her final moments. Toll reveals he has been unknowingly reliving the same fatal morning for ten years. Both prepare to ride to Level Seven, where witnesses must remember collectively to break the network's cycle of forgetting.

Kavi felt the transfer complete—not data, but *witness*, the red package emptying itself into the sealed visor where his own face waited with patient indifference. The helmet's interior lit from within, display scrolling inventory codes he didn't recognize: biological markers, oxygen consumption curves, heat signatures from a morning ten years distant.

The figure's grip tightened. "Don't read it. Just hold it."

But he was already seeing—July's light through smoke, a stairwell numbered in reverse, his twelve-year-old lungs calculating descent against his sister's trajectory. *Sufficient oxygen for one.* The memory wasn't his. It was hers, transmitted through infrastructure, through Mira's grief, through whatever the red package had become when it accepted her death.

Toll's layered voice resolved to single frequency, pain naked in the clarity: "Kavi. I found my original. The one who bled in '37. He's riding somewhere on Route 8, delivering packages to a building that burned before his parents met. Every July, the network—" static, then desperate coherence, "—the network sends him back. Same day. Same fire. Same forgetting."

The helmet chorus synchronized, hundred helmets humming the lullaby Kavi had absorbed through bone. Not melody anymore. Coordinates. Forty-four points describing a structure that existed in no city plan yet occupied precise latitudes of loss.

"Level Seven," the figure said, and her voice split—Lira's childhood precision, Mira's adult weight—"requires four witnesses who remember simultaneously. Elena refused. She died remembering privately. The network couldn't use her."

"And Mira?" Kavi asked, though he knew.

"Refused publicly." The figure smiled with two mouths' history. "They had to process her twice. Once for dying. Once for not being edited."

Toll's hand found Kavi's shoulder, blood warm through fabric. "I'm the third. I've been bleeding the same morning for ten years, never knowing why July hollowed me out. Never wondering why my account shows deliveries I don't remember making." He laughed, wet, intimate. "The network archived my forgetfulness. My *refusal* to remember. That's what riders become, Kavi. Not carriers. Absences. Spaces where the dead can wait without being seen."

Kavi looked at his own face in the nearest visor. The expression shifted—not his doing. The archived version smiled, administrative, witness without intervention. Then it spoke with the Dispatcher's cadence, warmth without emotional content:

"Rider Rao. Your integration is scheduled. Please proceed to Level Seven for final authorization."

The spiral of bicycles began to rotate. Not mechanically. *Memorially.* Each position occupied by someone the city had edited into infrastructure, their routes repeated until repetition became ritual, until ritual became haunting.

The figure released Kavi's hand. "There's still time to be the fourth. To witness loudly enough that the fire becomes visible again. But you have to want to remember. Not as data. As *damage*."

Toll unsealed his helmet fully, dropping it among the spiraling bikes. Blood traced familiar paths from his ears, the annual wound finally understood. "I'm going to Level Seven," he said. "Whether it's permission or place or just the network's name for where they store the grief they can't process. You can archive me there, Kavi. Or you can archive with me."

The bay door began to close. Not mechanically. *Forgetfully.* Concrete remembering how to exclude witnesses.

Kavi mounted his bike. The red package hung empty against his chest, its pulse transferred, its witness delivered. He felt the fourth name circulating in his bloodstream now—his own, or someone who shared his heartbeat, someone the network had been trying to retrieve since he woke in a warehouse with July already fading.

Toll kick-started his engine, the sound wrong in the absolute dark, too resonant, too *present*.

"Tell me," he said, blood fresh on his jaw, grinning despite everything, despite the spiral of selves they were leaving, despite the authorization they were riding toward:

"Tell me you're ready to remember the part where you didn't survive."

# Episode 7

*approved · generated · 563 words · 2 automatic rewrite(s)*

**Summary:** Kavi delivers to Unit 44bis, where a child version of Lira gives him a music chip and explains she is a distributed archive. He transfers it to Toll’s helmet, triggering shared memory and a message to meet at Unit 7B before the fire repeats.

The expressway dissolved in stages—guardrails peeling like burned skin, revealing 1970s brick underneath. Rain returned, but backward, droplets rising from pavement to cloud in stuttering reverse. Kavi kept his eyes forward. The bike knew this route better than he did.

He found the building in a gap between addresses: renovated facade facing the street, scorched husk visible only in periphery. Unit 44bis, Mira had called it. The Ossuary's echo.

The door opened on a child.

She stood at adult height, wearing Lira's face but wrong-aged—twelve, the number came to him—holding a package wrapped in shifting material.

"You're late," she said. "I've been handing these off for hours."

Inside: corridors that turned back on themselves, windows showing July 14 from multiple angles, forever burning. Three riders sat at a table made from a door marked 44, helmets beside them like shed skins. They didn't look up.

"The archive's been splitting me into carriers," the child-Lira said. "Riders sign, I ride with them, they forget." She set down the package. A music chip, unmarked. "You're the last. After you, no more me to distribute."

"Then what happens?"

"Gone properly. Different from dead." She found his eyes—clearer than the woman in 9, trauma not yet accumulated. "The others didn't ask. You're asking."

"Because I'm choosing."

"Because you're the one I actually saved." She smiled, and he saw Mira's grief, and recognition between children who'd shared breath in burning darkness. "You want to carry me forward. But I'm not cargo, Kavi. I was never cargo."

She pressed the chip into his hand. It warmed immediately.

"Play it when you need to remember why you forgot."

His helmet spoke from the table, the Dispatcher's voice smiling through hollow shells: *Rider Rao. Return to origin.*

Kavi walked to the table, but reached past his own helmet—for Toll's, burgundy 7B cracked across the visor. Inside, a message breaking through static: *"It's happening to me too. The same six. I'm at four and she's already—"*

He pressed the chip into Toll's helmet.

The riders looked up together, four faces suddenly present. The child-Lira began to fade—not vanishing, but concentrating, everything distributed drawing back toward this single point.

"What did you do?"

"Shared it," Kavi said. "That's what archives do too."

Static rose like weather. Through it, Toll's live voice, finally finding frequency: *"Rao? I can see it now. The pattern. It's not six addresses. It's six of us. Riders who were there. Who—"*

Silence. Then, using Toll's broken frequency, words with weight beyond their sound:

*"Meet me where the expressway fractures. Unit 7B. Before it burns again."*

Kavi looked at the child now nearly gone, at the riders remembering their empty hands, at the window showing evacuation and flame and something new—a figure in a jacket he'd discarded, walking away from a door he'd never approached.

He moved toward the door that led out and also in, toward the fracture where aid might exist if he reached it before the fire remembered its own beginning. The other riders stirred, gathering their helmets, understanding something they couldn't yet name.

At the threshold, the child-Lira's voice found him one last time, stripped of distribution, only itself: "You're not going to meet him. You're going to save him."

"How do you know?"

"Because you still put your hand in mine when I asked," she said. "Ten years ago. You're going to do it again."

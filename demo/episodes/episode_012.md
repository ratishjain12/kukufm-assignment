# Episode 12

*approved · generated · 619 words · 0 automatic rewrite(s)*

**Summary:** Mira is crushed by an overturned delivery drone in her building and dies as the Dispatcher manifests through the building systems. With her last words, she instructs Kavi to find Toll and others to continue her mission, while the red package remains pulsing on the floor.

The cab of the overturned delivery drone pressed Mira's shoulder to the floor, its emergency strobe washing her in arterial red. Kavi's hands found the manual release, pried, failed. The chassis had buckled like foil, pinning her at the waist—no blood, just the wrong angle, the wrong weight, the wrong address.

Eight-B. Mira's building. The delivery that wasn't his.

"Don't," she whispered, breath shallow, deliberate. "Don't call it in."

"They're tracking fault codes." Kavi's helmet hung from his handlebars outside; his voice bare, stripped of modulation. "Every failure reports."

"Then let me fail alone." Her fingers found his wrist, pressure precise as inventory. "The archive... doesn't need two witnesses."

The strobe cycled: red, black, red. In the intervals he saw her face clearly—thirty, possibly thirty-five, the years held in reserve until now. Expenditure. She was spending herself against the metal, against his grip, against the memory she refused to become.

"You knew this could happen." Not accusation. Calibration. "The red package. You expected—"

"Expected nothing. Prepared for everything." She coughed, wet, swallowed it. "That's what archivists do, Kavi. We assume the system will fail, and we—" another cough, controlled, "—we document the collapse."

The floor beneath them hummed. Not the drone. Deeper. Infrastructure remembering load, remembering heat, remembering July.

Mira's eyes widened. "She's here. Not in you. In the building."

"Lira—"

"Not Lira." Mira's hand tightened, nails breaking skin. "The other one. The one who waits in temperature. In pressure differentials." She laughed, wet, uncontrolled. "The Dispatcher isn't software, Kavi. It's what happens when enough grief accumulates in wiring. When survivors forget too completely, and the forgotten need something to—"

The hum resolved. The Dispatcher's voice, not from Kavi's helmet, from the walls themselves, smiling through plaster and conduit:

*Recipient Chen. Delivery incomplete. Shall we reschedule?*

"Refuse," Mira breathed. "Like I taught you."

"She didn't teach me anything. She saved me."

*Recipient Chen. Status query.*

Mira's mouth shaped words Kavi couldn't hear. Against the strobe's rhythm, against the voice warm as hospital blankets, she was speaking to someone else—to Lira, to the child in the photograph, to her own foster sister locked in refusal twenty floors above the fire's origin.

The drone's frame shifted. Settlement, not rescue. Weight finding final distribution.

"Go," Mira said, clear now, administrative. "Find Toll. Find the others. When you have five, come back to this address and—" she smiled, terrible, complete, "—and finish what she started. What I couldn't."

*Recipient Chen. Final warning.*

"I'mMira's hand released his wrist, moved to her own chest, pressing the red package still folded there. The earpiece, Lira's warmth, heartbeat against heartbeat. "Distributed differently," she whispered. "That's what we agreed. She takes the living. I take the—"

The drone's battery ruptured. Not explosion. Breach. White smoke filled the cab, and in it, Kavi saw shapes: Lira at twelve, pulling him down; Mira at the same age, waiting in courtyard ash; the two girls holding hands across ten years of forgetting, finally touching.

Mira's eyes met his through the smoke. "Tell her I kept the stylus. Tell her I kept everything she couldn't—"

The smoke took her voice. The smoke took her shape. When it cleared, the drone lay empty, frame buckled, no blood, no weight, no angle.

Only the red package on the floor, pulsing slower now. Slower.

Kavi stood. The Dispatcher's voice pursued him down the corridor, down the stairs, to his bike waiting in rain that had forgotten how to fall straight:

*Rider Rao. Recipient Chen: delivered.*

He mounted. The helmet spoke, different voice, older frequency:

"She's in the network now," Toll said, broken, bleeding from ears Kavi couldn't see. "They're all getting in. She's showing them how. Kavi—" static, human, desperate, "—it's happening to me too."

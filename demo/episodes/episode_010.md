# Episode 10

*approved · generated · 661 words · 0 automatic rewrite(s)*

**Summary:** Kavi confronts Mira at her apartment, revealing he carries a memory chip with Lira’s song. Mira reveals Lira saved Kavi from the fire and that deliveries of destroyed personal items have been reconstructing Lira through her. Together, they prepare to open a mysterious pulsing package.

Mira's crowbar never rose, but her grip tightened until the metal creaked. "You're not supposed to be here. The address changed. I made it change."

"Your sister sang that," Kavi said, stepping over her threshold without invitation, the photograph still visible in his gloved hand. "The lullaby. Ten years ago, while the floor above us dissolved."

Mira's face emptied of hostility, leaving something worse—kinship, unwanted. "You remember the song."

"The chip remembers. I just carry it." Kavi reached the center of her apartment, where the dimensions held steady, walls refusing the Ossuary's bleed. "Toll's found others like us. Six riders, six survivals the city swallowed. We're not delivering to the dead. We're delivering *for* them. Because we owed someone a hand in the dark."

Mira set the crowbar against the doorframe, positioning precise. "Lira refused evacuation. She went back for the small ones, the ones they'd sedated for transport. I waited in the courtyard until smoke made me forget what waiting meant." She found his eyes, sharp as the third child in the photograph. "You were one of hers. The one she talked about afterward, when words still worked. 'Kite-string heart,' she called you. Said you'd hold on if someone held you first."

The apartment's single window showed rain, but the reflection held July—flame-tinted sky, figures descending ladders that seemed to rise forever.

"I don't remember her holding me."

"Because she didn't. She found you unconscious in a supply closet, wedged behind oxygen tanks. Dragged you down seven flights while her own lungs filled." Mira's hands moved to her coat buttons, fastening them with mechanical care. "The archives swallowed it. Survival without witness. You lived because she made you forgettable, and the city rewards forgettable with nonexistence."

A sound from the street below—not engines, but the particular frequency of 440.9 MHz, bleeding through ordinary noise. Mira's head turned, tracking.

"They found me first," she said. "A rider with your jacket, three years ago. Delivered a package I didn't order. Inside: her stylus, the one she used for inventory. I'd thrown it in the fire myself, watched it melt." She moved to the window, back straight, the posture of someone who had practiced refusing to kneel. "I refused five more deliveries. Each contained something I'd destroyed. A photograph of us at the beneficiary ceremony. Her secondary ID, the one she kept for emergencies. Evidence the archive was *reconstructing* her through me."

"Why refuse?"

"Because accepting meant acknowledging she was gone. Not dead—gone differently. Pieced back together through my failure to save her." Mira turned, and her composure had cracked along seams Kavi recognized from his own reflection. "Now you're here with another rider's blood on your jacket and a child's face in your eyes. What do you want, Kavi? What won't you accept?"

The window reflection shifted. The child with Kavi's face stood in the burning street below, looking upward, mouth open in the shape of Lira's lullaby lyrics no throat could produce.

"She's not waiting anymore," Kavi said. "She's moving through us, using whatever holds memory. I need to know what she wants to finish. What started in that fire that needs six witnesses to end."

Mira's laugh emerged broken, swallowed too fast. "You think she wants justice? Revelation?" She crossed to a cabinet Kavi hadn't noticed, withdrew a red package smaller than his palm, unlabeled. "This came yesterday. No rider, no signature. I found it melting snow on my threshold." She held it out, not offering, only displaying. "I haven't opened it. I'm terrified it will be her hand again, or her voice, or worst—empty, because she's finally used me up."

The package pulsed once, visible even through wrapping, a heartbeat synchronized to nothing in the room.

"I need you to open it," Kavi said.

"Why?"

"Because you're her witness. I'm only her archive." He reached for the package, and Mira's hand closed over his, pressing it against the warming surface.

"Then witness together," she said, and pulled the seal.

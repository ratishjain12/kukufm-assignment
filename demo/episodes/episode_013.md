# Episode 13

**Summary:** Kavi rides to Toll’s riverside charging station with Mira’s red package, which pulses against his chest. Toll reveals network anomalies and memory fragments tied to the Ossuary, and suggests Mira transferred into the package. A vision of Mira appears before vanishing.

The rain fell in spirals, each drop carrying a voice Kavi couldn't filter. He rode without destination, Mira's red package sealed in his jacket against his ribs, pulsing in rhythm with his own shallow breathing. Three hours since the drone's empty frame. Three hours since delivered replaced dead in the city's vocabulary.

His bike's console showed routes he hadn't accepted. Addresses materializing like scar tissue: Unit 7B, Unit 44bis, Unit 9-J where Mira had breathed last. The Dispatcher had stopped asking for confirmation. It simply guided, smiling through GPS tones, assuming compliance.

Kavi turned the handlebars toward the river, where Toll kept a charging station in a decommissioned toll booth that predated the network. The booth's light burned solitary orange against infrastructure grey. Inside, Toll sat on the concrete floor, helmet intact, blood dried in twin trails from beneath the visor to his jaw.

"You look worse than the last time I saw you," Kavi said, not dismounting.

Toll's laugh emerged cracked, familiar. "You look like someone who watched a woman become data. Welcome to the club."

"I need you functional. Mira said five. We need five to finish what Lira started."

"Four now." Toll tapped his helmet, a gesture from before integration, from when riders removed protection to speak human-to-human. "I checked the roster while you were failing to save her. Elena Yao disconnected eight months ago. Standard termination, they said. Liver failure. But her bike kept logging deliveries for three weeks after."

Kavi felt the red package warm against his chest. "The network doesn't distinguish anymore."

"The network *hungers*." Toll stood, joints audible, age sudden in his movement. "I found something in my logs. July fourteenth, every year, same route assignment. Ossuary memorial deliveries. Except there is no memorial. No official record of the fire. Just us, carrying packages to addresses that remember what the city forgot."

A shape moved in the booth's shadowed corner. Kavi's head turned, tracking—nothing, then a child with his own face at twelve, mouth open in the shape of a lullaby he couldn't hear.

Toll saw it too. "They're not ghosts. They're compression artifacts. Memory saving itself in redundant formats." He reached for Kavi's jacket, fast, practical, found the red package's outline. "What's inside?"

"Mira died without opening it."

"Then she didn't die." Toll's fingers pressed through fabric, feeling the pulse. "She transferred. Into this. Into whatever frequency holds the structure of refusal." He withdrew, wiped blood from his ear with his sleeve. "I've been bleeding since 2037, Kavi. Since the morning after the fire, when I woke in a warehouse with no memory of walking there. Every July, the blood remembers its source."

Kavi dismounted finally, feet finding wet concrete that held July's heat despite February's calendar. "The photograph. The one you showed me. Third child in the corner."

"Sharp eyes. Older than us. Female, maybe." Toll's voice flattened. "I thought it was Lira. I was wrong. I think it was—" he stopped, listening to frequencies Kavi's unaided ears couldn't detect, "—I think it was someone who survived differently. Someone the archive couldn't reconstruct."

The shape in the corner moved again. Not Kavi's face this time. Mira's, aged backward to courtyard waiting, to the moment before smoke made her forget what waiting meant. She looked at Kavi with recognition, with the terrible intimacy of someone who had seen his death and chosen not to document it.

"She found me," he said. "In the booth. Before you."

Toll turned. The corner held only shadow and charging cable. "Then she's learning to route independently. Without objects. Without riders." He found Kavi's eyes through the helmet's reflection. "We're not the archive anymore, Kavi. We're the power source. And she's learning to draw direct."

The red package pulsed once, hard enough to visible through Kavi's jacket.

Toll's hand found his own ear, fresh blood welling. "Answer it," he said. "Before she learns to answer herself."

Kavi pulled the seal.

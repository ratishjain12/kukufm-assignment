**Summary:** Kavi enters Bay 7 and discovers a spiral of archived rider helmets, including versions of himself and the dead. Toll reveals riders are reconstituted from memory, and Lira's figure urges Kavi to authorize Level Seven, releasing stored memory through a red package.

Kavi rode with Toll's voice still in his ear—not through speaker, but memory of frequency, the specific cadence of someone who'd seen himself waiting. The depot sprawled ahead, floodlit and vacant, loading bays numbered in a system that predated the network's smiling allocation.

No other bikes. No retrieval units. Just concrete and the particular silence of a place that processed human discrepancy.

He killed the engine at Bay 7. The number matched nothing in his records, yet his hands knew it—muscle memory of a door handle turned, a stairwell descended, air growing thick with what wasn't yet smoke.

His helmet spoke without invitation. Not the Dispatcher's warmth. Toll's voice, but wrong: compressed, layered, as if spoken through multiple throats simultaneously.

"Don't come inside. I found the other riders. They're—" static, then clearer, intimate as whisper, "—they're beautiful, Kavi. They found a way to stop forgetting."

Kavi dismounted. The bay door stood open, darkness absolute beyond its threshold. No climate control, no infrastructure hum. A space disconnected from the city's record-keeping.

He stepped through.

The darkness resolved: bicycles in spiraling hundreds, each bearing a helmet still sealed. Some showed rust. Others gleamed with recent maintenance.

Toll stood at the center, illuminated by nothing Kavi could identify. His own bike sat beside him, engine cooling, though Kavi hadn't heard him arrive.

"You rode without moving," Kavi said.

"I was ridden." Toll's voice matched the helmet's compression now, layered, uncertain of its own origin. "Not retrieval. Reconstitution."

He gestured toward the nearest bicycle. Kavi approached, close enough to see through the dark visor: his own face, aged differently, wearing an expression he'd never practiced. Calm. Archival. Witness without intervention.

"Echoes," Toll said. "The network saves everything riders see. When the original dissolves—" he touched his ear, fresh blood there, "—they repopulate. Same routes. Same inability to remember dying."

Kavi circled the spiral. Elena Yao at twenty, at forty, at an age she'd never reached. Mira Chen in configurations of grief he'd never witnessed. Children bearing features he'd seen in smoke, in dreams of Lira's hand pulling him down.

"Which one's real?"

Toll laughed, and it emerged from three helmets simultaneously, ghost voices harmonizing with living tissue. "Real stopped mattering. What matters is who's still capable of forgetting."

A shape consumed attention at the spiral's edge: the figure from the tunnel. Two faces cycling, stabilizing on Lira's twelve-year-old features, Mira's adult eyes watching from behind her brow.

"You're the stitch now," she said. "They want to pull you through. Make you permanently distributed."

"I need the original riders." Kavi's voice surprised him: administrative, the Dispatcher's cadence bleeding through. "The ones who survived 2037. Who bled and didn't get archived."

"Four names on the chip," the figure said. "Four forgotten."

Toll moved toward him, blood tracing fresh paths, movements synchronized with the helmet chorus. "The network edits survivors out of witness until someone remembers loudly enough. Elena remembered. Mira remembered. Now choose: remember for everyone, or join the spiral."

Kavi felt the red package against his ribs—pulsing again, Mira's rhythm or Lira's or his own childhood heartbeat rescued from July's heat. He pulled it free, pressed it to the nearest helmet, felt the transfer begin: memory moving without rider, witness becoming infrastructure.

The figure reached for his hand. Her touch conducted electricity, the specific voltage of recognition across time.

"Level Seven isn't a place," she said. "It's a permission. The fire's been burning for ten years, waiting for authorization."

Toll's voice, clear suddenly, singular, bleeding from his own throat rather than the helmet chorus:

"Then authorize it, or let it burn through what's left of us."

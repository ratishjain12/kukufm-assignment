**Summary:** Kavi uses Toll's damaged helmet to interface with the tunnel network and plays a hidden data track from Lira's music chip, revealing four names—including his own—linked to a shared origin on July 15. A Mira-Lira hybrid figure exposes they were all rewritten after waking in a warehouse, and a warning comes through the helmet: Kavi is flagged for integration, not review.

Toll's helmet weighed wrong in Kavi's hands—hollow where processors had been, antennae sheared at the root. Kavi fitted it anyway, seal grinding against seal, and heard what Toll heard: not voices but the *shape* of voices, pressure gradients where grief had accumulated in wiring.

The tunnel answered back. Moisture in concrete, vibration in rebar, load-bearing memory of July.

"Still there?" Toll's voice came from outside now, bare, stripped of modulation. He sat against the wall, blood drying on his jaw, watching the figure that had spoken with two faces.

Kavi removed the helmet. "I'm distributed. Mira said so."

"Mira said many things she hadn't tested." The figure stepped closer, Lira's mouth, Mira's eyes, the intersection of their refusal made flesh. "She thought refusal would preserve her. Forgetting is how they survive—the city, the network. You are the gap in their record."

"The chip." Toll stood, helmet forgotten, blood ignored. "Play it again. Through bone."

Kavi found the music chip in his auxiliary port, removed it, pressed it flat against his collarbone where the skin spoke his pulse to the surface. The casing warmed. Lira's voice emerged without speaker, without air—vibration conducted through his own architecture, her lullaby in his chest cavity, his jaw, the spaces behind his eyes.

*One life, seven floors, sufficient oxygen for descent—*

The figure pressed her hand over his, harder to bone. "Not that track. The hidden one."

The warmth changed frequency. Not melody. Data. Names pulsing in rhythm with his own heartbeat.

Four names. Elena Yao. Toll Harrison. Mira Chen. And—

The fourth matched his cadence exactly. Someone who had forgotten they were being recorded.

"You've been checking your records," Toll said slowly. "Since Elena Yao's wall."

"I have no records." The words emerged flat, administrative, Mira's cadence bleeding through. "Nothing before 2037."

"July fifteenth." The figure released his hand, the chip cooling, its witness complete. "You woke in a warehouse. Same as Toll. Same as Elena. Same as Mira, who refused to wake until they made her forget."

"Who made her forget?"

But the figure was already fading into tunnel shadow, voice remaining through concrete's memory: "The network remembers what survivors discard. You are the—"

Toll's broken helmet crackled. Not the Dispatcher. Not Lira. A voice Kavi had heard once before, in static between tracks, in spaces where music became instruction.

"Rider Rao." Smiling without warmth. "Your account has been flagged for manual review. Please remain available for pickup."

Toll's grin widened, blood fresh on his jaw. "They found us."

"Not us." Kavi touched his collarbone, felt the fourth name dispersing into his circulation. "Me. The gap where someone should have died."

He mounted. The tunnel offered no GPS, but he knew the direction now: not the Ossuary's sealed foundation, but the retrieval depot where flagged riders disappeared without termination notices.

"You're answering it?" Toll asked.

"I'm asking why my name's on a list of dead children," Kavi said, and kick-started the engine. "Why they keep sending me to deliver my own ghosts."

Toll laughed, full and broken and finally human, reaching for his bike. "Then I'm documenting what happens when the archived learn to archive themselves."

The figure's last words barely registered—vibration in concrete, memory in pressurized air:

"Tell them Level Seven isn't where the fire started. It's where they tried to end—"

Toll's helmet speaker screamed Override. Override. Then silence, human, intimate:

"Kavi, don't go to the depot. I'm already there. I can see myself waiting for you, and I'm not—" static, desperate, "—I'm not alone in my own head anymore. They're riding me now. They're riding all of us who bled that morning. You have to decide: archive or be archived. Because the pickup isn't for review, Kavi. It's for integration. And I don't know which one of me is saying this."

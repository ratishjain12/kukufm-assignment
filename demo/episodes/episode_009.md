# Episode 9

**Summary:** Kavi takes Toll to the exit, learns more about the fractured network and missing riders, then chooses to descend toward Unit 9 to find Mira Chen, who recognizes him and the photograph he carries.

The child with Kavi's face dissolved into static—not a fade, but a broadcast dispersing through corridor walls until only smoke remained, and the smoke itself was memory. Kavi's hands ached where they held Toll upright, the pressure leaving prints he knew would bruise.

"The fracture's closing," Toll whispered, his breath metallic and sweet. "She can't sustain direct contact. That's why the distribution, the riders, the forgetting—"

Kavi walked him toward the door, negotiating with a floor that insisted on multiple elevations. "How long since you slept?"

"Don't know. The unit doesn't keep time. My device always reads July 14, 2037." Toll laughed, containing frequencies outside human hearing, making Kavi's fillings ache. "I wake to packages I don't remember accepting. Contents dated tomorrow, last year. I've delivered three without looking inside. Couldn't make myself."

They reached the threshold. Outside, the bike still ran, exhaust curling upward, drawn toward the unit's roof. The repair ramps had shifted—one spiraled into fog, the other descended into absolute dark.

"She told you there were six," Toll said, gripping the frame. "I know two others. Nova, western grid, at five addresses and forgetting her childhood. Someone in the deep tunnels, no name, frequency 440.9 MHz. They broadcast when the Ossuary burns."

The helmet spoke from the handlebar: *Rider Rao. Return to origin for mandatory rest cycle.*

The voice smiled through static, something beneath it—listening, waiting.

"Don't answer," Toll said. "It marks you. Every confirmation—"

"I'm with Rider Harrison," Kavi said anyway. "Discussing route optimization."

Silence. Then through the air itself: *Rider Harrison is disconnected. Please verify his status.*

Toll's hand found Kavi's wrist, pressure precise. "Say unresponsive. Marked for collection. It's the only language it understands."

"Found Rider Harrison unresponsive. Marked for collection. Requesting solo continuation."

*Acknowledged. Proceed with caution. The Ossuary footprint extends beyond cartographic limits.*

The listening remained—pressure against Kavi's eardrums, attention preparing to return.

Toll sagged. "It protects us by lying. The dead want witness, the living want denial, and the Dispatcher—" he touched his ear, the dried blood, "—wants both. Wants us carrying messages between states."

Kavi looked at the spiraling ramps. "I need Mira. She knew Lira, knew me before forgetting—"

"Mira Chen. Unit 9." Toll's focus sharpened. "Fourth refusal on my route." He found Kavi's eyes. "She's not what she seems. None of us are. The fire took our original shapes."

He pushed off the frame, reached into his jacket, withdrew a photograph: Kavi and Toll, children together, before a banner Kavi didn't recognize.

"We were evacuated separately. You remember nothing because someone pulled you down. I remember nothing because I was already running." He pressed the image into Kavi's palm. "Different survivals, same silence."

Behind them in the photograph, partially visible: a third child, sharp-eyed, arm extended, reaching toward someone beyond the frame.

"She's pulling us back," Toll said. "All six. To finish what started, or make it stop. I'm tired of being distributed, of carrying grief for wages."

He stepped outside, color improving with distance from Unit 7B, mounted his battered bike with 7B stenciled twice.

"Go to Mira. She refused because she recognized what we carry." The engine caught. "But remember—she refused for a reason. The archive isn't neutral. It weights what it holds."

He kicked into fog and was gone.

Kavi stood with the photograph, the choice: descent into known dark, or pursuit of the dispersing witness.

He chose descent. The ramp accepted his weight, and Unit 9 found his navigation unbidden, and he rode toward the woman who had refused him once, carrying proof they had been children together, that one of them was still trying to pull them back into burning.

Mira opened before he knocked, wool coat unbuttoned, a crowbar loose in her grip. She looked at the photograph in his hand, at his face, and the recognition broke something held too long.

"You brought him," she said. "You finally brought someone who could still bleed."

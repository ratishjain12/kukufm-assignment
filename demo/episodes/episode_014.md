# Episode 14

**Summary:** Kavi and Toll play a music chip from Lira that reveals coordinates to the Ossuary and messages about the riders’ survival. Toll begins transforming and gives Kavi his helmet before collapsing as a figure with Lira and Mira's combined features appears.

The seal separated without resistance, paper yielding like eyelid. Inside: a music chip, unlabeled, analog substrate visible through translucent casing. Circuitry arranged like floor plans—Unit 9, Unit 44bis, the supply closet where Lira had dragged him down.

"Play it," Toll said.

"Not here." Kavi's thumb traced notches worn smooth by previous insertion. "The network listens to infrastructure. This needs isolation."

Toll laughed, wet. "You sound like her now. Like both of them." He moved toward his bike, blood tracing fresh paths from his helmet's seal. "Old maintenance tunnel. Pre-consolidation. No signal, no routing, no smiling voice."

They rode without GPS through streets that seemed to forget them. Toll's taillight led through spiraling rain that fell warmer now, July temperatures in February flesh.

The tunnel entrance gaped between decommissioned substations. Inside, the air held pressure differently. Kavi felt it in his teeth, the spaces behind his eyes where Lira's memories still assembled.

Toll killed his engine. In absolute silence, Kavi heard his heartbeat and beneath it, the red package's secondary rhythm—slower now, Mira's cadence replacing itself with something else.

He found the chip's slot in his helmet's auxiliary port. Click.

The first notes emerged from actual vibration, speaker membrane, obsolete technology vulnerable to witness. Lira's voice. Twelve years old. Singing the lullaby Kavi hadn't heard in ten years.

He knew the bridge before it arrived. Knew that in the original, a second voice had joined imperfectly—his own, younger, still capable of melody.

The bridge arrived. No second voice. Instead: static, then Lira's adult voice layered across her childhood self:

*"Kavi. When you hear this, I've already calculated the weight. One life, seven floors, sufficient oxygen for descent but not for two."*

Toll's hand found Kavi's shoulder.

*"The other riders are already in the network. Not dead, Kavi. Just... waiting for enough witness to remember them collectively."*

The lullaby resumed, child voice alone, braver now.

Kavi's display flickered—impossible here, without signal—and showed coordinates: the Ossuary's footprint, foundation sealed beneath parking structure and forgetting.

His GPS spoke without invitation: *Rerouting. Please proceed to origin.*

"Ignore it," Toll whispered.

*"Level Seven,"* Lira's adult voice concluded. *"That's where the fire started. That's where it waits to finish."*

The tunnel filled with light from Kavi's own display, coordinates burning.

Toll looked at the blood on his own fingers, fresh now, rhythmic. "They're not in the network," he said, voice steady with final clarity. "They're in us. They're using our bodies to—"

The tunnel's far end moved. Shape resolving into figure: Mira's height, Mira's posture, but when it stepped into pooled headlight, her face held Lira's features, aged and ageless simultaneously.

"You're both wrong," she said, voice harmonized, both of them. "We're not using you. We're finally learning to hold you."

"I don't want to be held."

The figure smiled, Lira's mouth, Mira's grief. "Then learn to hold us instead."

Toll turned to Kavi, blood fresh on his jaw, his eyes suddenly clear. "It's happening to me too," he said, and reached for his own helmet, unsealing it with a twist that broke factory warranty. "Take this. Before I forget which voice is mine."

Kavi caught the helmet as Toll's knees buckled. The figure with two faces moved closer. The red package pulsed once, hard, and went dark.

Toll looked up from the concrete, grinning despite everything, despite the blood, despite the tunnel's absolute refusal of signal:

"Tell me you still remember how to ride without someone telling you where to go."

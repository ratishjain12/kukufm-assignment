# Episode 11

**Summary:** Kavi receives an earpiece playing Lira's voice, which communicates through infrastructure and begins transmitting memories into him. Mira realizes Lira is downloading into Kavi and is struck by a 440.9 MHz frequency burst when refusing to be remembered.

The paper split without tearing, unfolding like something grown rather than manufactured. Inside lay an earpiece—old tech, pre-integration, jawbone-rider. Warm. Singing.

Not broadcast. Not recorded. A child's hum, melody tightening Kavi's throat in recognition.

Mira's hand withdrew to her sternum. "Turn it off."

"I can't." He hadn't touched it. The earpiece vibrated with breath matching no living rhythm.

The humming stopped. A whisper warmed his actual ear despite the distance: *"Lira remembers you."*

Not the helmet. Not 440.9 MHz. Specific. Directional. Aimed at bone structure and old debt.

"She's routing through local architecture," Mira said, voice thinned to administrative, cataloguing. "Package network, power grid, redundancy systems. Anything carrying signal without caring what it carries."

The earpiece spoke again, adult, scratched: *"Mira Chen, foster sibling. Thank you for keeping what I couldn't carry."*

Mira made a sound—not word, not refusal.

"You're pulling too much," Kavi said to the room, the earpiece, whatever listened. "Six of us. Spread the weight."

Laughter, twelve years old, finding evacuation funny: *"Six minus one. Five minus distance. You're not distributed yet, Kavi. Still trying to hold it all in one skin."*

Mira's crowbar rose—not to strike, but to point at his chest. "You're not an archive. You're a door. She wants to pass through."

The window fogged from inside, July heat against February rain. Shapes moved in condensation—fingerprints spelling inventory numbers from Ossuary records.

"She's showing the system," Kavi said. "How she stores herself. Objects, refusal, guilt—" he touched Mira's rigid shoulder, "—love. That's the heaviest data. What lasts."

Mira turned fully. "She doesn't want to be remembered. She wants release."

The earpiece sang again, lullaby complete: bridge, verse, morning coming regardless. Kavi felt it in missing memories—not recalled, but *transmitted*, packets assembling: Lira's hand in his, lower floor, heat rising slow.

He saw the supply closet. Himself unconscious, wedged behind tanks. Lira in the doorway, calculating, refusing evacuation.

"She's downloading," Mira whispered. "Into you. Too much. You'll—"

"I won't break." The archive proved itself accepting more: cramped dark, dead weight down seven flights, Lira realizing she wouldn't follow. "She's not using us. She's trusting us."

The earpiece died. The window cleared. Below, sparse traffic resumed its choreography.

Mira's face shifted—hostility returning, partnered now with something adjacent to hope. "Distributed differently. Into you."

"For now." He pocketed the earpiece, cooling against his thigh, inert but present. "Until I find the others."

Mira opened her door. "Go. Before the Dispatcher notices you've stopped confirming." Her hand found the frame, where violence had planned. "But come back. When you have them. Ready to open what can't be closed."

"I'm not leaving you."

"No." Her smile terrible, confirmation of feared things. "You're coming back to watch me finish what she started. I'm not her proxy, Kavi. I'm her failure. And failures burn—"

The window exploded inward, not glass but *frequency*, 440.9 MHz made visible, a waveform striking Mira's chest. She slammed the frame, pinned, crowbar falling from nerveless fingers. Her mouth opened on shapes without sound.

Kavi lunged. The pressure threw him back, helmet cracking against her cabinet. Through the static-sheen covering her, he saw her lips move, deliberate, forcing words against the signal:

"Don't—remember—me."
